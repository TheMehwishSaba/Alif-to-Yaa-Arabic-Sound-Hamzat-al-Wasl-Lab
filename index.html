1. The core rule: Arabic cannot start on a sukuun

Arabic syllables open with one consonant plus a vowel (CV). A word whose first real letter is silent (sukuun) would begin a consonant cluster, which the language does not allow. The Helper Alif is inserted at the start as a prothetic vowel (a temporary vowel added before the word). When another word comes before it, the helper is elided and the earlier sound flows straight into the sukuun.

2. Particles: only "al-" (ال) gets the Helper, always with fatha (a)

The helper carries the silent laam. Moon letters keep the laam (ٱلْقَمَر al-qamar); sun letters assimilate it into a doubled sound (ٱلشَّمْس ash-shams). Connected: وَٱلْقَمَر wa-lqamar, وَٱلشَّمْس wash-shams.

3. Nouns: Kasra (i) by default, a closed list

The famous seven (kasra, because kasra is the default vowel for nouns lacking "al-"); the same holds for verbal nouns of the longer verb forms, e.g. اسْتِغْفَار.

4. Verbs: the 1-2-3 Radar Rule (commands)

Letter 1 is the Helper, letter 2 is silent, letter 3 is the target. 3rd letter has damma → helper takes damma. 3rd letter has fatha or kasra → helper takes kasra. Beware the four fake dammas below. Longer verbs such as اسْتَغْفِرْ follow the same radar and give kasra.

5. The Ninja Warning: four fake dammas

When the 3rd letter shows damma only to match a plural و, the original vowel was kasra, so the helper stays kasra.
